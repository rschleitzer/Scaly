What runs here

One Scaly program, compiled to WebAssembly. Its work is a single for over the points of the picture. Nothing in it says “in parallel” or “on the graphics card”: the rounds of the loop do not depend on each other, so the runtime may hand them to other cores; and its body is arithmetic on 32-bit numbers, so the compiler also writes it as a kernel the card can run. What changes between the buttons is only what the host gives the program — one core, all of them, the card — and the picture comes out the same. relaxed before the loop allows the card's floating point, which rounds a little more loosely: on the card a few points in a thousand come out a shade different, and one on the very edge of the set may fall to the other side. The table says how many.

“Let Scaly decide” gives the program both: the runtime measures the first rounds on the processor and sends the loop to the card where that pays — a small picture with few iterations may stay on the cores. The times are those of the loop alone, measured by the program; the first run on the card includes compiling its kernel, and the first on the cores starting their threads.
